Grassroots Computing in C-U
Where is grassroots computing in a micro-urban college town in the United States?
Lily Rybka | INFO390 Grassroots Computing Final Project

May 4, 2026

Please note: this project is an initial exploration or mapping and that many other sites and initiatives of grassroots computing remain unmapped.
The header image of the Mosiac browser logo, in 1992, Mosaic was developed at the National Center for Supercomputing Applications (NCSA) at the 
University of Illinois at Urbana–Champaign (UIUC), and is the first browser to display images inline with text instead of a separate window.
﻿

The Champaign-Urbana area is a relatively high-tech community with widespread digital access. Today, around campus and the surrounding communities, most people carry smartphones and laptops. Yet the technologies most Americans use remain black boxes. Users rarely understand how they work, and these tools are standardized and distributed through a heavily top-down process.

In this context, grassroots computing appears in small yet significant ways. It surfaces in technology education, open-source software, repair cafés, makerspaces, mesh networks, posters, bulletin boards, volunteer groups, and anywhere people are encouraged to take things apart and put them back together.

In a community saturated with advanced academic and commercial technologies, grassroots computing is the work of understanding and unraveling technology and its relationship to the social fabric. It takes shape in spaces that may not seem technological, and wherever organizing and relationships are nurtured.
